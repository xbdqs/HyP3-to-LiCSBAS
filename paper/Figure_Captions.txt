Figure 1. Processing architecture of HyP3-to-LiCSBAS. Source HyP3 GAMMA ZIP products remain read-only. Preflight and planning are completed before expensive raster conversion; the accepted plan is then reused for common-grid phase/coherence conversion, baseline and ancillary reconstruction, validation and export. The lower audit lane indicates the machine-readable records retained across the workflow. LiCSBAS2 quality control and inversion remain downstream of the adapter.

Figure 2. Semantic mapping between ASF HyP3 GAMMA inputs and LiCSBAS2-ready GEOC outputs. Blue rows denote raster/phase transformations, green rows ancillary or viewing-geometry transformations, orange denotes network reconstruction, and purple denotes metadata/provenance handling. The mapping distinguishes quantities that are warped directly from those that require common-reference subtraction, least-squares baseline inversion, or look-vector reconstruction. Unsupported metadata are not fabricated.

Figure 3. Small-baseline network used for the MGX production validation. The stack contains 182 Sentinel-1A ascending acquisitions and 504 interferograms from 26 December 2019 to 30 December 2025. Edge colors distinguish 12-, 24- and 36-day temporal baselines. Perpendicular baselines are expressed relative to 15 January 2023 (orange star), which is used only as the baseline datum and ancillary reference. The open circle marks the degree-one acquisition on 5 September 2022. The graph contains one connected component and has cycle rank 323.

Figure 4. Network-wide phase-reference harmonization diagnostics for the MGX stack. (a) Hexagonal density of the 504 pair-specific HyP3 reference coordinates; 499 coordinates are unique. The orange star marks the common adapter reference at 126.6676°W, 65.3359°N. (b) Distribution of the additive phase shifts applied to place all interferograms on the common datum. The shaded band denotes the central 95% interval and the dashed line denotes the median. (c) Reference-window phase dispersion versus median coherence, colored by temporal-baseline class. All 504 interferograms satisfied the reference-window validity criterion.

Figure 5. Internal consistency of acquisition-level perpendicular-baseline reconstruction. (a) HyP3 pair baselines plotted against pair baselines reconstructed from the least-squares acquisition solution. The dashed line is 1:1. (b) Reconstruction residuals plotted by interferogram midpoint date; orange points exceed 5 cm absolute residual. The full-rank solution (181/181) has RMS residual 0.0155 m, median absolute residual 0.00077 m and maximum absolute residual 0.151 m. These residuals diagnose internal network consistency and are not an independent measure of orbit accuracy.

Figure 6. Production-readiness and portability validation. (a) Readiness gates for the real 504-interferogram MGX run, followed by numerical diagnostics for LOS-vector structure and baseline reconstruction. (b) End-to-end platform/backend tests. The real Windows run completed through the standard-library plotting fallback after a broken Matplotlib/Pillow dependency was detected; Linux synthetic tests passed with Matplotlib, stdlib and automatic backend selection. Plotting fallback affects network-figure presentation only and does not change scientific rasters or metadata.
